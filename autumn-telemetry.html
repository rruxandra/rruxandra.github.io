Autumn
Telemetry

I exported around 4 million data points over several years and asked an LLM what is wrong with me in autumn.

scroll
exhibit a · the disappearing sun
01

The model began by auditing the ambient light sensor hidden inside my watch. Between August and late autumn on the Swiss Plateau, my recorded daily daylight drops five- to nine-fold, while days that never once reach circadian-anchoring brightness multiply more than tenfold.

When I protested that this October was actually warm and sunny, the machine got meaner: at my Alpine latitude, even a cloudless October sky loses over 90% of my after-work daylight, leaves me waking in the dark, drops cool enough for a jacket sleeve to blind my wrist sensor, and tilts the sun low enough to wipe out virtually 100% of UVB.

5× to 9× less wrist light · 0% autumn uvb · one very smug parser
exhibit b · the 4 a.m. mutiny
02

Next it cross-examined several years of sleep. Total duration barely budged — varying by under 2% across the seasons, with virtually zero weekend social jetlag — yet autumn still left a quiet physiological tax:

Pulse
resting and sleeping heart rates climb 6.5% to 7.6% from August into November while nightly vagal recovery drops nearly 10%, every single year.
04:00 AM
pre-dawn wakefulness clustered noticeably following late-day workouts, compounding the seasonal dip in restorative sleep.
Winter sleep
across monitored winters, sleep conditions consistently degrade in sealed, heated bedrooms as dry air and indoor heating tax nighttime recovery.
Airways
sleeping breathing rate nudges upward from its August floor into Heizperiode winter air.
same hours in bed · −9.4% vagal recovery · late workouts compound pre-dawn wakeups
exhibit c · the accidental control group
03

When I tried to blame work or existential depth, the parser actually conceded half my alibi. Despite near-zero weekend social jetlag and a completely flat sleeping heart rate all seven nights of the week, weekdays consistently carry a measurable physiological surcharge compared with weekends — a workweek sympathetic tax as large as winter itself.

During several sunny, sea-level holidays, the telemetry revealed a consistent reset: sharply increased daylight exposure pushed resting heart rate down below even a restful weekend at home despite a substantial surge in daily walking; back at northern latitudes, the seasonal baseline returned immediately.

weekday physiological surcharge · sea-level holidays · work and latitude neatly separated
the verdict · narrative vs. photobiology
04

The Somatic Collimator argued that mood is the fiction a nervous system writes when it cannot read its own telemetry. Autumn at northern latitudes is the grandest example we have: for centuries we have composed nocturnes and romanticized spleen over a seasonal drop in melanopsin signaling and 25-OH Vitamin D.

I gave an algorithm four million chances to tell me I was a complex autumn creature. It informed me I am functionally a slightly dramatic houseplant parked too far from the window.

not a dark night of the soul · just unwatered chlorophyll
the prescription · my Alpine latitude

I don't need to listen to music in autumn and be sad. I just need 10,000 lux and Vitamin D.

Turn on an obnoxiously bright light before breakfast, take the Vitamin D, and stop mistaking a photon deficit for character.
