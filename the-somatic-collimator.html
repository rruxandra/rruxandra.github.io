The somatic collimator / a follow-up

What if every signal carried enough data to be read exactly as it is, and every system had enough sensors to act on it precisely?

What if the nervous system were perfect?

Not more resilient. Not wiser. Perfectly calibrated. Enough sensors, enough resolution, enough data to pinpoint exactly what every signal means and route it to the precisely correct action. No ambiguity. No narrative. No gap between sensing something and knowing what it is.

This is not an impossible thought experiment. It's the logical end of the trajectory we're already on. Every biosensor, every wearable, every diagnostic model, every real-time analytics pipeline moves us one step closer to a world where signals don't need to be interpreted; they only need to be recognized.

The body as API

In a perfectly calibrated world, the body is an API. A glucose drop is a glucose drop: not anxiety, not discipline, not longing. The system reads the signal, identifies the deficit, and acts. The narrative never forms.

Signal: low serotonin precursor → Action: targeted amino acid intake.
The fridge loses its emotional gravity. No more comfort food.

Signal: prefrontal cortex glycogen depletion → Action: sleep trigger sequence.
The feed loses its hypnotic gravity. No more losing two hours to an algorithmic trance.

Signal: cortisol spike from social threat → Action: boundary adjustment.
Not a pill, not a doctor, not panic. The psychosomatic pathway never opens.

Comfort food is a misrouted signal: fuel need dressed as emotional need. Perfect calibration undresses it. Doomscrolling requires fatigue to be ambiguous enough to mistake for curiosity; clarity breaks the trance. Half of chronic pain is the nervous system misclassifying social threat as tissue damage. Resolve the classifier, and the pain dissolves.

Zero-packet-loss semantics

If every message came with full telemetry (tone, intent, emotional state, context), there would be nothing left to misread. No subtext. No passive aggression. No poetry.

Subtext is a compression artifact of low-bandwidth communication. Full-duplex emotional telemetry makes it obsolete. Ghosting only works because silence is ambiguous; in a calibrated world, silence has a return code. The love letter exists because longing requires a gap between signal and fulfillment. Close the gap; lose the letter.

Metaphor as lossy compression

Art exists in the gap between what a signal is and what it might mean. Perfect calibration closes that gap. Would anything remain to create?

Metaphor is lossy compression of a state that lacks a direct channel. Build the channel; lose the poem. Music is the scenic route to a neurochemical destination. With direct access, who takes the long way home? A cathedral is a building where the excess (the light, the height, the silence) is the point. Calibration has no concept of excess.

The debugging epiphany is the most interesting loss. That aha moment in the shower, the elegant hack at 3am: it's a misclassified background thread completing. It's a signal that was too noisy to read in real time, so it resolved itself offline and delivered the answer as a flash of insight. Beautiful. But in a calibrated world, the fault is identified, the patch is applied, and you never get to feel that particular joy.

Latency as substrate

The Somatic Prism showed that one signal becomes many worlds. This follow-up asks: what if you collapsed all those worlds into one? What if meaning were never constructed, only recognized?

The answer is unsettling. You get a world of extraordinary efficiency and zero unnecessary suffering, but also a world without art, without longing, without the particular human experience of not knowing what you feel and building something in the space of that not-knowing.

Every poem is a routing failure. Every love letter is a latency artifact. Every cathedral is an unoptimized allocation. Every startup is a patch for a network that hasn't finished indexing.

The gap between signal and meaning is where most of human culture lives. It is wasteful, painful, and the source of most of our confusion. It is also the only place where anything truly new can be made.

A perfectly collimated world would work flawlessly. But would it matter?
